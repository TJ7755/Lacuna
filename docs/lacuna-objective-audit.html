Is Lacuna optimising the right quantity?

Six auditors put the four hypotheses to the code and to executed sweeps. One adversarial reviewer then tried to destroy all six. Two of the four hypotheses did not survive contact — and the most serious defect in the system was not one of them.

30 July 2026 branch codex/arc6-audio 6 auditors · 1 adversarial verifier read-only · no repo writes
Fact-checked 9 August 2026

This historical audit has been corrected against the current code and primary FSRS sources. Numerical sweeps and simulations whose scripts, seeds and raw outputs were not retained are labelled unreproduced, not verified. They may be genuine, but the report does not contain enough evidence to reproduce them.

Verdict

The objective is defensible; some inputs to it are not. Every hypothesis in the brief targeted the estimator — the shape of ΔR, its aggregation, its horizon. The estimator is broadly sound. The clearest lower-layer defect is that machine-marked and ordinary recall response times are pooled despite measuring different things. Machine-marked grading is structurally three-valued — Again, Good and Easy — although Hard is unreachable because selfCorrected is hard-coded false.

The single most consequential finding was not asked about. Every auditor measured the expectedMarks branch of the objective. The securedTopics branch is thresholded and discontinuous, which makes it far more sensitive to exactly the optimistic-grade assumption the brief was interrogating — and nobody tested it.

1 · The estimator

bias confirmed, remedy rejected
As briefed

ΔR-given-Good is not E[ΔR]. The expected value is R·ΔR_good + (1−R)·ΔR_again. R is already available and deterministic, so the dependency-free property survives the change.

What survived

The bias is real, systematic, and monotone in R. The proposed correction is not the right replacement — it is a two-point model of a four-point grade space, and it discards 24.5% of the probability mass.

Unreproduced executionReal modules claimed

The bias is systematic, not marginal reordering

Sweeping the real forwardSim across horizons and stabilities: rank correlation between the two quantities never drops below 0.993 and pairwise inversions stay under 2.6%. But corr(R, gap) sits between −0.95 and −0.99 at every horizon, and the gap scales four to five times from strong cards to weak ones. The metric consistently over-values weak cards, and the effect roughly triples as the horizon lengthens.

E[ΔR] as specified replaces one mis-specification with another

A two-branch expectation over Good and Again omits the Hard and Easy transitions, including the w[15] and w[16] effects. A sign flip is reachable — but only under optimised weights

The first sweep found zero sign flips in 3,500 cells and correctly scoped that to default weights. The verifier re-swept 3,600 cells under weight vectors the optimiser is permitted to install. Flips appear, concentrated on badly overdue, low-stability, high-difficulty cards — leeches. CLAMP_PARAMETERS allows w11 down to 0.001, so w11 = 0.1 passes validateFittedWeights unmodified. A user who optimises their deck can install weights under which serving the top-ranked card is expected-harmful.

Traced in codeUnmeasured

The branch nobody tested is the one most exposed

securedTopics is not a smooth marginal gain. It scores −1 when the card is already above threshold, 1 + rNo when a review would push it above, and rYes otherwise — a discontinuous jump at the 0.90 line, driven by a rYes that assumes Good. Rank correlation measured on the expectedMarks branch says nothing about it. An optimistic rYes pushes cards across the band boundary, and a discrete misclassification is a far larger error than a small ranking gap.

Reframed

The starvation hypothesis inverts, then dissolves

Weak cards are not outbid — the bias above favours them. There is no anti-starvation mechanism (no round-robin, no aging, no due-date gating in the Learn session, and newCardsPerDay unset means unlimited), but the surviving vector is new cards out-ranking review cards, because a never-seen card has rNo = 0 and therefore a large ΔR. The verifier's rebuttal stands: under the stated objective a never-seen card genuinely is the highest-marginal-gain item. That looks wrong only because a new card's true P(Good) is far below a review card's — which is the Good-assumption again, not a separate defect.

2 · Correlated siblings

refuted
As briefed

Sequences generate overlapping cloze cards from one ordered definition; FSRS treats them as independent. Does the exam-day forecast multiply per-card recall across siblings?

What survived

No. Nothing multiplies. The aggregate is an arithmetic mean, the distortion on a real course is 0.6 points, and the quantity is display-only — no decision reads it.

Traced in codePremise refuted

The multiplication never happens

averagePredictedRetrievability is an arithmetic mean; masteryFraction is a threshold count. A naive independent product for a 20-item sequence at R = 0.9 would report 0.12. The app reports 0.90. The failure mode the brief anticipated is absent from the codebase.

Unreproduced executionSeverity overstated

The headline number refutes its own severity claim

The 11.8-point overstatement comes from a single hand-built 20-item sequence with an assumed serial-position dip — a distribution chosen so that mean and min diverge. On a realistic 420-card mixed course the gap is 0.6 points, because clusters are roughly a tenth of the pool. The uniform-R case is degenerate: mean, min and geometric mean all coincide at 0.90.

Traced in code

Nothing downstream reads it

The mean does not feed scoreCard, and session completion tests masteryFraction or raw ΔR. The only decision the number could influence is "stop studying", and it is 0.6 points off. The comparative-reading defence in the brief is technically wrong — mean and min are not monotone transforms of one another over time — but it does not matter, because no decision depends on the difference.

Traced in codeReal, unrelated

No sibling burying exists — but the fix is authoring, not modelling

Eligibility, cooldown and session assembly were read in full. The only spacing mechanism is keyed on card id and fires only after a failed grade. Items 14, 15 and 16 of one sequence can appear in a single sitting. However: if each generated card is separately gradable, they are independent marks and linearity of expectation is correct. A sibling discount is right only if the exam scores the sequence all-or-nothing, and nothing in the data model records which. That is a per-note authoring flag, not a change to the objective.

Corrections

Two corrections to the prior audit

Cloze is not a sibling case at all — one card hides every deletion simultaneously, so there is exactly one FSRS card per note. And the reversible-pair creation site is share.ts:865-879; the previously cited 767-773 is the packing-side mirror detector, which does not create cards.

The simulation refutes the complaint it was run to support

Choosing the mock costs +0.002 R on the final — nothing. The app's default path, unselected practice, already resolves each card to its nearest applicable future checkpoint, so the default behaviour is already near-optimal. Blindness is reachable only by an explicit learner override, documented as deliberate and non-retained at SPEC.md:416. Attack (c) lands: this respects a stated preference. Arguing otherwise is close to arguing the learner should not be allowed to choose.

Traced in codeMagnitudes unreliable

The 4.9-point cost is directionally sound and numerically soft

The simulation grades success as Good and failure as Again, so it inherits the exact assumption section 1 interrogates. Because both arms share that policy and the headline result is a near-tie, the direction survives the circularity. The magnitudes do not. The 35.5-expected-marks figure should not be quoted in a remediation plan.

Traced in codeSurvives

Two genuine local defects

Selecting an assessment builds the exam-date context with an empty assessment list and empty lesson map, discarding per-card resolution — so a card covered by an earlier checkpoint than the selected one receives a horizon that is too far out. That is an internal inconsistency with the unselected path, not a preference. Separately, once the selected date passes the practice pool returns empty with "You are caught up" and no rollover to the next covering assessment. Both are small, local and worth fixing.

Traced in code

No clamp, and no disclosure

Horizon feeds ranking and mastery only; it never touches the due date, which comes from ts-fsrs driven by grade and stability. Intervals can run past a covering assessment. The picker offers "Revise for {assessment}" under "An upcoming assessment overlaps material you have reached", with no overlap indicator and no statement of what the choice costs. Given the measured cost is one-directional, a disclosure is only warranted on the final-selecting branch.

4 · The grade the scheduler receives

the real damage
As briefed

Synthesised grades versus the human 1–4 distribution FSRS-6 was fitted on; per-deck calibration pooling card types; contamination of the review log by known auto-marker bugs.

What survived

The distribution comparison is a category error. The narrower defect is still real: Hard is unreachable, Easy remains possible only for full marks under three seconds, and machine-marked production times are pooled into the ordinary recall calibration.

Category errorVerifier

Distribution mismatch is not, by itself, a defect

FSRS conditions its transitions on review history and grade, so a different marginal grade histogram alone does not prove bias. That statement requires stable grade semantics and conditional relationships, however. Lacuna manufactures grades from response time and machine-awarded marks, so semantic and measurement shift remain legitimate concerns even when the marginal histogram proves nothing. The previously quoted 5.2-million-row corpus and exact grade percentages had no traceable authoritative source.

Prior audit refutedVerified independently

The timer does not run through feedback — and the replacement claim is also wrong

The response time is frozen at the question-to-answer transition, before any grade button exists, and a comment in the source says so explicitly. Self-graded timing is clean. But machine-marked timing is not "inflated by feedback" either — its guard requires the question phase, and the time is computed on the first line of the handler. Nothing leaks feedback time anywhere. The actual defect is a units mismatch: machine-marked time measures answer production (typing several lines of working) while self-graded time measures recall latency, and both are folded into one per-deck pool with no card-kind gate. Any remediation written against "the timer keeps running" would fix nothing.

Synthetic scenarioMechanism confirmed

Pooling incompatible timings can collapse the adaptive thresholds

Pooling ordinary recall latency with typed-answer production time is a real defect. Under the synthetic component moments assumed below — recall mean 5s and typed-answer mean 60s — the Easy threshold becomes negative and the Hard threshold rises sharply. Those assumptions were not measured from Lacuna users, so the table demonstrates a possible failure mode, not the current production distribution. Machine-marked grades bypass these adaptive thresholds; the contamination affects later ordinary self-graded reviews. “Always Good” was therefore too absolute.

Machine-marked grading emits three of the four grades

selfCorrected is hard-coded false at both call sites, so grade 2 is dead code for machine-marked items. Full marks in under 3.0 seconds do produce grade 4, and the test suite asserts that boundary explicitly. The function therefore emits {1, 3, 4}. Easy is probably rare for substantial typed work, but it is not structurally unreachable.

Traced in codePartially mitigated

Self-optimisation would absorb this, if it ever ran and if the labels held still

A stationary grade stream can be refitted, but “absorb” was too confident: aggregate calibration does not guarantee that one parameter vector handles heterogeneous card types or changed grade semantics. Parameters exclusive to absent grades are unidentified and harmless only while those grades remain absent. The optimiser still needs 1,000 pooled reviews and runs only from settings, while the lifetime calibration window changes composition as the deck's card mix changes.

Traced in codeCorrected derivation

The raw grade delta is not the complete difficulty transition

The raw delta is −w[6]·(g−3), but FSRS then applies linear damping, mean reversion and bounds. Good can reduce difficulty when the current value lies above the reversion target, and Again need not increase it at the upper boundary. The earlier “ratchet” conclusion was false because it treated one term as the complete transition. Lacuna delegates the full update to ts-fsrs.

Traced in codeDecisive

Contamination is identifiable but not recoverable

The review log is append-only; undo reverses only the immediately previous attempt. Affected rows can be located exactly from the stored marks fields. But the grades cannot be recomputed: selfCorrected has no field on the log at all, and the stored response time is the mismatched primitive rather than a clean input badly derived from. One genuine remedy exists — the per-deck performance record is a pure Welford fold over history, so it can be deterministically rebuilt, and rebuilt while excluding machine-marked entries from the pool.

Remediation, ranked

what the verifier would actually fix

  1. Gate the response-time pool by card kind, and resolve the dead Hard branch

    Typed-production times are pooled with recall latencies and can distort the adaptive thresholds used by later ordinary reviews. Separately, selfCorrected is hard-coded false at both call sites — wire it or delete it, but do not leave a grade branch that cannot fire. The per-deck performance record is deterministically rebuildable from history, so the calibration record can be rebuilt while excluding machine-marked timings.

  2. Measure the Good-assumption on the securedTopics branch before changing anything

    The thresholded, discontinuous branch is far more sensitive to an optimistic rYes than the smooth branch every auditor measured. Report band-misclassification rate, not rank correlation. This is the one place where the brief's original hypothesis is likely to be understated rather than overstated.

  3. Guard against expected-harmful serves under optimised weights

    A sign flip is unreachable under FSRS-6 defaults but reachable under weight vectors the optimiser is permitted to install. The cheap guard is independent of whatever the primary ranking uses: refuse to serve a card whose expected ΔR is negative. This does not require adopting E[ΔR] as the ranking metric.

  4. Restore per-card horizon resolution when an assessment is selected, and handle the passed date

    Two small local defects: the empty assessment list discards per-card resolution so cards covered by an earlier checkpoint get too distant a horizon; and once the selected date passes the pool empties with no rollover. Neither is "the model blends assessments" — fix them as the narrow bugs they are.

Do not put these in a plan
  • The sibling aggregation. 0.6 points on a real course, display-only, and no decision reads it. If sequence-level grading matters, that is a per-note authoring flag, not a change to the objective.
  • "Our grade distribution differs from the corpus." A marginal histogram mismatch alone does not establish bias; changed grade semantics or conditional relationships still can.
  • "Assessment horizons should be blended." The default path already picks the nearest checkpoint, choosing the mock costs nothing on the final, and the override is a documented deliberate design position.
  • E[ΔR] as a two-point expectation. A four-grade replacement needs a defensible per-card grade-probability model. A global corpus histogram is not one.
